Drive safely. Pull over only where legal. Stay on public roads and public property. Do not enter active industrial sites, landfills, fenced areas, rail corridors, vacant lots, or private property.

Before you begin

This is a landscape tour, not a contamination detector.

The app points to documented places and explains what happened there. A site can have a cleanup history and still carry restrictions, capped material, buried contamination, institutional controls, or unresolved questions.

“Remediated” does not mean contamination-free.

Location access is optional and used only in your browser to estimate which stop is closest. The app does not need an account and does not send your location to a Toxic Niagara server.

What the route reveals

A regional network — not a single source.

Radioactive slag used as fill could have come from more than one industrial source. Federal and state investigations later separated some road and property anomalies from Manhattan Project waste and linked others to industrial slag beneath asphalt, driveways, parking lots, and roads.

The driving tour keeps those histories together because that is how the landscape works: factories, aerospace research, haulers, disposal areas, road fill, creeks, neighborhoods, and later cleanup programs overlap.

Evidence

Built from the record.

Each stop is backed by government records, federal worker-exposure documentation, cleanup records, aerospace histories and reporting used in Greetings from Niagara. The source metadata stays in the project data rather than interrupting the driving-tour narrative.

The app is structured so photographs, archival documents, audio, and expanded evidence can be added stop by stop without rebuilding the project.
